BACKGROUND

How ABHD was defined: names from discovery, motifs from consensus sequences

Names

LABH1–3 → ABHD1–3
BAT5 → ABHD16A
C13orf6 → ABHD13
CGI-58 → ABHD5
Family ABHD1–16B, at least 19 genes

Motifs

Elbow: small–X–Nu–X–small, GXSXG in most ABHDs
Acid after strand β7; His invariant
HX₄D: “acyltransferase” motif

Inferred activity

Ser-His-Asp: esterase, lipase, phospholipase
HX₄D: acyltransferase (ABHD5: LPA)
Neither motif: unknown (ABHD15)

The edges: ABHD5 has Asn for Ser, ABHD15 lacks the elbow and HX₄D. Both stay in the family by sequence similarity.

Our check: the HX₄D histidine is the catalytic His, so the motif marks the His loop, not a separate site. It is weak evidence of acyltransferase activity (next slide).

Sources: Lord, Thomas & Brown 2013; Ghosh et al. 2008; Montero-Moran et al. 2010

Names: LABH1–3 were lung α/β hydrolases; BAT5 is HLA-B-associated transcript 5; C13orf6 is a chromosome 13 open reading frame; CGI-58 is “comparative gene identification 58”. The 2013 review lists ABHD1–16B (at least 19 proteins). Motifs: the nucleophilic elbow follows strand β5 and carries the nucleophile (Ser, Cys or Asp); the review gives the consensus small–X–Nu–X–small, which is GXSXG in most ABHDs. The acid (Asp or Glu) usually follows strand β7 and the His is invariant. The HX4D motif was previously associated with acyltransferase activity: ABHD1, ABHD3, ABHD13, ABHD14A and ABHD16B lack it per the review, ABHD15 has neither it nor an elbow. Activities: ABHD5 has no hydrolase activity (Asn replaces the nucleophilic Ser), co-activates ATGL, and acylates lysophosphatidic acid in vitro (Ghosh 2008; Montero-Moran 2010). The acyltransferase assignment rests on an HX4D consensus conserved in CGI-58 orthologues and on homology to yeast Ict1p; the motif was not mutated in either paper. Our own check (next slide): in ABHD5 and ABHD4 the HX4D histidine is the catalytic His of the triad, so the motif is the His loop, not a second site.
